Side project: learning hardware by building
Kawasemi Rover: Building an Outdoor Quadruped
to Learn Robot Hardware from the Ground Up
An original 12-DoF legged robot, designed in CAD, 3D-printed and taken outdoors
Masahiro MutoPersonal project (planning and early design)
WHY My research uses off-the-shelf robots, so the mechanics, motors, power and wiring stay a black box. By taking a 12-DoF quadruped all the way from CAD → 3D printing → control → outdoor tests myself, I want a hands-on understanding of how robot hardware works.

1What I am building

An original outdoor quadruped that draws on the mechanics and control of Stanford Pupper v3. The goal is to walk on grass and gravel and to patrol, explore and perceive its surroundings. It is not a copy of Pupper v3: the body, mechanisms and software are my own design.

6Designing one 3-DoF leg

Hip ab/adduction, hip flexion/extension and knee. One leg comes first, to check calculations against measurements.

p = f(q),  ṗ = J(q) q̇
M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + g(q) = τ + JcTfc

Kinematics in MATLAB, torque τ in Simscape Multibody, used to pick actuators.

7Sizing the body for the terrain

Flat ground, grass, gravel, steps and slopes each favour different leg lengths and body sizes. Before detailed CAD, I choose the dimensions in simulation.

Inspired by RoboGrammar (SIGGRAPH Asia 2020). I do not search over topologies: the 12-DoF quadruped layout is fixed and only dimensions are optimised.

ParameterAffects
Thigh / shank lengthStride, steps, torque
Hip placementLeg spacing
Body / CoMStability on slopes
Foot shapeContact area, slip
Joint rangePosture and fall recovery

Evaluation loop

  1. Generate URDF / MJCF models from the parameters
  2. Build flat, bumpy, gap, slope and low-friction terrain in MuJoCo
  3. Optimise a gait controller (MPC or RL) per candidate
  4. Compare speed, energy, fall rate and joint torque
  5. Pick within actuator and 3D-printing limits

8Outdoor tests

  • Walking speed on flat ground, grass and gravel
  • Climbing small steps, posture on slopes
  • Battery life, joint temperature, number of falls

The first robot targets dry, flat ground with small bumps. Mud, rain and steep slopes come later.

9Roadmap

  • Phase 1: one legSizing study, Fusion design, IK and torque estimates, joint control from Python→ an original 3-DoF leg
  • Phase 2: the quadrupedBody, four legs, battery layout, IMU, ROS 2→ a 12-DoF quadruped
  • Phase 3: RL walkingURDF and MuJoCo models, PPO, Sim2Real→ a robot that walks with RL
  • Phase 4: outdoor mobilityBetter feet and shell; grass, gravel, steps, slopes→ an outdoor-ready quadruped
  • Phase 5: autonomyRGB-D, LiDAR, SLAM, Nav2, outdoor patrol
  • Phase 6: VLA and Agentic AILanguage-driven exploration, failure detection and recovery

Now in early design. Three actuators for one leg first; twelve once they prove out.

10Cautions

  • Safety first. Joint and torque limits, an e-stop, first tests on a stand
  • Cost. Roughly ¥200k–600k, mostly the twelve joint actuators; validate one leg before scaling
  • Licences. Check each Pupper v3 repo, CAD file and firmware before reuse

A hardware-learning side project, separate from my main research on vision-tactile failure prediction. Phase 6 may try the same failure-recovery ideas on legs.
